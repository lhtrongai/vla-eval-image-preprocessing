\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are ranked by their success rates on simulated benchmarks such as LIBERO, often by margins of a few points. Before a policy sees a camera frame, the evaluation code processes it with steps copied from the training data pipeline: in the official OpenVLA evaluation, a JPEG round trip, a Lanczos resize and a center crop. A re-implementation can drop or change any of these steps without raising an error. We change one step at a time and measure the closed-loop success of two released OpenVLA-family checkpoints, OpenVLA-7B and OpenVLA-OFT, on all \NPairs{} initial states of LIBERO-Spatial, pairing every episode with the reference episode that starts from the same state (\MainEpisodes{} episodes in \NCells{} cells). The primary comparison and its decision rule were written down before the \NPairs-episode variant cells ran, but after a pilot on \PilotPairs{} of the same initial states that included two variant steps, and they have no public timestamp.\HookAbstractPlan{} Turning off the JPEG round trip changes OpenVLA-7B by \SevJpegDiff{} points (\AlphaPct\% interval [\SevJpegLo, \SevJpegHi]; \OutDiff{} on the \OutPairs{} states outside the pilot); the one-sided upper bound on the loss, \SevJpegUB{} points, is above the \ThresholdPts{} points fixed in advance, so a loss of that size is not ruled out. No secondary comparison is significant after Holm correction. The small net changes hide many flips: every variant changes the outcome of \SevFlipsMin{} to \SevFlipsMax{} of the \NPairs{} OpenVLA-7B episodes, in both directions, while an identical rerun on the same stack changes \SevRerunFlips. \HookAbstractCancel{} In an exploratory open-loop check on identical observations, every step changes the predicted actions of both policies, and a changed first action is not followed by more flips. We release the per-episode logs, the evaluation code and the analysis scripts, which regenerate every number in the paper.
\end{abstract}
