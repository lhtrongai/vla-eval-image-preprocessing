\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are ranked by their success rates on simulated benchmarks such as LIBERO, often by margins of a few points. Before a policy sees a camera frame, the evaluation code processes it with steps copied from the training data pipeline: in the official OpenVLA evaluation, a JPEG round trip, a Lanczos resize and a center crop, applied to frames rendered at $\RenderRes \times \RenderRes$. A re-implementation can drop or change any of these steps without raising an error. We change one step at a time and measure the closed-loop success of two released OpenVLA-family checkpoints, OpenVLA-7B and OpenVLA-OFT, on all \NPairs{} initial states of LIBERO-Spatial, pairing every episode with the reference episode that starts from the same state (\TotalEpisodes{} episodes; the primary comparison and its decision rule were fixed before any variant was run). Turning off the JPEG round trip changes OpenVLA-7B by \SevJpegDiff{} points (\AlphaPct\% interval [\SevJpegLo, \SevJpegHi]); the one-sided upper bound on the loss, \SevJpegUB{} points, is above the \ThresholdPts{} points fixed in advance, so a loss of that size is not ruled out. No secondary comparison is significant after Holm correction. The closest, the center crop on OpenVLA-OFT (\OftCropDiff{} points), is exploratory because it rests mostly on one task; a rerun of its cell on the host CPU of the other OpenVLA-OFT cells repeats all \NPairs{} outcomes.
The small net changes hide many flips: every variant changes the outcome of \SevFlipsMin{} to \SevFlipsMax{} of the \NPairs{} OpenVLA-7B episodes, in both directions, while an identical rerun on the same stack changes \SevRerunFlips. This extends to the evaluator's own image steps the cancellation of opposing outcome changes that paired evaluations have reported for compound perturbations. With that many flips, a pipeline change with no systematic effect still moves a \NPairs-episode score within about $\pm$\SevBandMin{} to $\pm$\SevBandMax{} points. In an exploratory open-loop check on identical observations, every step changes the predicted action on many observations, by jumps between action bins for OpenVLA-7B and, for OpenVLA-OFT, by under one bin on average along the rollouts except when the crop is off; we find no association between a changed first action and a flip. We release the per-episode logs, the evaluation code and the analysis scripts, which regenerate every number in the paper.
\end{abstract}
